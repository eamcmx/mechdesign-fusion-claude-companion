Write a Fusion Python script that, when run, builds each machine, takes out of it the part its checks changed most
(the five of design_office/lib/drawings_detail.py) and makes that part's engineering drawing, with on each sheet what
the checks and design_office/lib/materials_fits.py decided for it, so that a shop that never sees the model makes the
part that passed the checks; and a sheet of the bag gripper's opening stop put together, which carries what the fitter
must do.

INPUTS (a command dialog, with this default):
- the part: one of all, bracket, segment, spacer, fitting, disc or stop, all

DERIVED (never typed):
- design_office/lib/drawings_detail.py: the five parts and the stop's items, their numbers, their machines, and every
  value a sheet carries
- design_office/RangeSweep/RangeSweep.py's machine(): each machine as its detail script builds it; for the tool
  changer, MaterialsFits.apply() first, which the other four builds already run
- the part by its name in the machine; its classes and roughnesses from the attributes
  design_office/MaterialsFits/MaterialsFits.py left on its faces; its material from
  design_office/lib/materials_fits.py

HARD CONSTRAINTS:
- Build each machine through RangeSweep.py's machine(), loaded, not copied.
- Take the part's body out into a design of its own, in the part's frame: z along its largest cylinder's axis, x
  towards its centre of mass, the origin on that axis at the body's lowest point.
- Carry into the model what the checks decided for its shape before drawing it: the opening bracket's arm turned 45
  deg about its own axis by projects/palletizer/StopBracket/StopBracket.py's own function, loaded, not copied, then
  its root filleted 2.5 mm on its four edges; the same in the opening stop's assembly, so that its bracket is the one
  its own sheet draws.
- Save each design to the cloud folder design_office/lib/drawings_detail.py names, or the one build() is given, under
  '<number> <title>', and never over a file already there; ask Fusion for its automatic ISO drawing (A3, millimetres),
  asking again while the design is not yet ready; close the auto-dimension dialog it leaves open.
- Read the classes off the faces; do not decide them again.
- On each sheet, find the curves by their geometry, not by their order:
  the bracket: the two bolt holes' size, their distance between centres, a position frame of diameter 1.0 at maximum
  material from datum A, the foot's bolting face;
  the segment: datum A its top face, datum B its 15 deg face; each wedge face's profile of 0.02, the 15 deg face's
  from A and the 5 deg face's from B, in its table (a profile frame from the API stops Fusion's drawing command);
  the spacer: the bore's size; datum A the face on cone A and the other face parallel to it within IT5, in its table
  (a datum on a round part's end face stops Fusion's drawing command);
  the spar fitting: the bores' size where they show round, and the bolt holes';
  the stop disc: the bore's size.
- The opening stop: its six kinds of item (the bracket, the DYSC-25 and its lock nut, the M8 screws, washers and nuts
  of jaw A's opening bracket) as one component each with its part number and designation, a piece of each where the
  machine has it, in the bracket's frame; Fusion's assembly sheet with its parts list; a table with the seat, the
  screws' torque and preload of design_office/lib/fatigue_detail.py, and the absorber's lock nut.
- Write on each part's sheet a table of requirements, one row each with the source that decided it: the material, the
  rows of drawings_detail.rows() and the classes read off the faces, the general tolerance ISO 2768-mK.
- The API gives the table no place, and Fusion sets it over a view on the segment's, the spacer's and the disc's
  sheets: tidy those three sheets with Fusion's own Tidy Up (Sheet.tidyUp) before the PDF.
- Export each sheet as a PDF to design_office/results/drawings/<number>.pdf and write what was made, added and read
  back to design_office/results/part_drawings.json.
- Refuse a part that is not one of the six.

FUSION CONVENTIONS:
- A new design document (FusionDesignDocumentType) for each part and one for the opening stop; no joints in them,
  as-built or otherwise: a part is one body, and the stop's items stand where the machine has them. The machines they
  come from keep their own scripts' joints (the wrist's, the flap's and the axle's As-built joints) and are closed.
- ISO drawings, first-angle projection, millimetres; the API's cm in the model, mm on the sheet.
- Fusion's automatic drawing leaves its auto-dimension command open, its preview dimensions on the sheet: end that
  command (terminateActiveCommand) and wait until the sheet's count of dimensions stays the same before counting what
  is there or adding to it.
- Read each view through its viewCurves, in sheet millimetres: an arc's or a circle's radius is the distance from its
  startPoint to its centerPoint. Fusion picks the views' scale: take it as the largest radius on the sheet over the
  part's largest radius. A hole shows round in a view where a curve's radius is the hole's radius times that scale,
  to 0.05 mm; dimension it there with addDiameterDimension.

ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA:
- A message gives each drawing's views, dimensions, symbols and PDF, the folder, and a version tag.
- Six drawings, each A3, each with its PDF on disk: a part's one sheet of at least two views, the stop's two sheets
  (Fusion's view with the parts list, then the projections).
- Each sheet gains the dimensions and symbols asked of it, counted on the sheet after the script.
- The bracket's model has four faces of 2.5 mm radius at its root and two holes of 9, and its arm turned: its volume
  within 0.01 % of design_office/lib/lugs_brackets.py's; the stop's bracket its arm turned and its root filleted the
  same.
- The stop's six kinds with their pieces, and its torque row.
- Every file in the folder once.
- Refused: a part called crane.
